“The winners won’t hate wrappers. They’ll master them.”
“It’s just a wrapper” — The most misused phrase in AI right now. Just when I thought the “it’s just a wrapper” noise couldn’t get louder, a16z drops the take I’ve been quietly chewing on for months.

If a lawyer tells you they’ve built a legal AI product, don’t roll your eyes and call it a wrapper.
Ask the real questions instead:
- Does it actually work at scale? 
- What’s the engineering? 
- How have they cracked the compute economics? 
- Is the workflow genuinely better than the old ways? 

Because the difference between a toy and a defensible product almost always sits in that “wrapper.”

I spent 3 hours fighting withto build one presentation… and thendid it in 5 minutes.

Yesterday was painful.

I had a clean project setup, a high-quality concept note, and extremely detailed prompts. Everything was structured exactly how I wanted.

#ChatGPTfollowed instructions perfectly. But that’s all it did.

No spark. No improvisation. No “let me push this one level higher for you.” It felt like working with a uninspired junior who only executes — never thinks ahead. Prompt after prompt, edit after edit, until I slammed my laptop at midnight in frustration.

This morning, fresh Claude quota in hand, I recreated the same project. Same concept note. Similar instructions.
Five minutes later — voilà.
Not just done. Done beautifully. With the exact ingenuity and taste I was craving. The difference was night and day.

Here’s what I’m observing as someone who lives deep in the AI trenches — 
The hype cycle has quietly shifted.

ChatGPT, once the undisputed king, is slowly slipping into the sea of average copilots. Mediocre.
Claude (Anthropic) is eating everyone’s lunch when it comes to deep reasoning, creative improvisation, and actually understanding intent.

My current rough ranking for serious work:
Claude — clear leader # 1
Gemini — surprisingly capable in many cases at # 6
# 7-10. ChatGPT, Grok, Copilot

The bigger point isn’t just which model wins today.
It’s that the gap between “following instructions” and “showing real intelligence” is becoming the real differentiator — especially for AI-native founders building products that actually feel intelligent.
